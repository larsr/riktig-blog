The Clifford sign is a cocycle, and that is why the product is associative

Take a space with basis vectors e1,e2,e3e_1, e_2, e_3 and one rule: distinct basis vectors anticommute, eiej=−ejeie_i e_j = -e_j e_i, and each one squares to a number you fix in advance, eiei=sie_i e_i = s_i. Everything else follows.

Multiplying two products of distinct basis vectors is then a mechanical procedure. To compute e1e2⋅e2e3e_1 e_2 \cdot e_2 e_3, slide the two copies of e2e_2 together — one transposition, so one factor of −1-1 — then replace e2e2e_2 e_2 by s2s_2:

e1e2⋅e2e3=−e1e2e2e3=−s2e1e3e_1 e_2 \cdot e_2 e_3 = -\,e_1 e_2 e_2 e_3 = -\,s_2\, e_1 e_3

Three things happened, and they are independent of each other. The set of surviving basis vectors is the symmetric difference of the two input sets — the ones appearing in exactly one of them. The number out front is a sign, from counting transpositions, times the product of sis_i over the basis vectors that appeared in both. Write AA and BB for the two sets. Then

eA⋅eB=σ(A,B)eA△B,σ(A,B)=(−1)#{transpositions}∏i∈A∩Bsie_A \cdot e_B \;=\; \sigma(A,B)\; e_{A \mathbin{\triangle} B}, \qquad \sigma(A,B) = (-1)^{\#\{\text{transpositions}\}} \prod_{i \in A \cap B} s_i

Now the question. Is this product associative? Multiplying out (eAeB)eC(e_A e_B) e_C and eA(eBeC)e_A (e_B e_C) gives the same set both ways, since symmetric difference is associative. The coefficients are another matter: the left grouping counts transpositions for AA against BB and then for A△BA \mathbin{\triangle} B against CC, and the right grouping counts BB against CC and then AA against B△CB \mathbin{\triangle} C. These are different countings. Associativity is the claim that they come out equal:

σ(A,B)σ(A△B,C)=σ(B,C)σ(A,B△C)\sigma(A,B)\,\sigma(A \mathbin{\triangle} B, C) \;=\; \sigma(B,C)\,\sigma(A, B \mathbin{\triangle} C)

That equation has a name, and it is not specific to this situation at all.

The equation, on its own

An abelian group BB and a function σ\sigma from pairs of its elements to the nonzero scalars, satisfying exactly the displayed equation plus σ(0,a)=σ(a,0)=1\sigma(0, a) = \sigma(a, 0) = 1, is a two-cocycle on BB — the same notion that classifies extensions of BB by the scalars in group cohomology. Written as a class, with the function as a parameter:

  Class Cocycle (F B : Type) {eF : EqRel F} {oF : One F} {mF : HasMult F}
      {eB : EqRel B} {zB : Zero B} {aB : HasAdd B}
      (sigma : B -> B -> F) := {
    cocycle_proper       :: Proper (equ ==> equ ==> equ) sigma;
    cocycle_equation     : forall a b c : B,
                             sigma a b * sigma (a + b) c
                             == sigma b c * sigma a (b + c);
    cocycle_left_normal  : forall a : B, sigma 0 a == 1;
    cocycle_right_normal : forall a : B, sigma a 0 == 1;
  }.

The group is written additively, so + is the group operation — for the blades it is symmetric difference — and 0 is the identity, the empty set. Nothing in the class mentions basis vectors, transpositions or a signature.

Given any such σ\sigma, the elements of the group can be multiplied with the coefficient in front. A general element is a family assigning a scalar to each group element, and the product is a convolution:

  Definition twist (x y : B -> F) : B -> F :=
    fun d => bigopI (fun a => sigma a (a + d) * (x a * y (a + d))).

Read the coefficient at dd: it sums over every aa, pairing aa with the unique partner a+da + d whose symmetric difference with it is dd, and weights the two coefficients by σ\sigma. In the group of blades this is the Clifford product, once σ\sigma is the sign times the signature product.

The Clifford sign satisfies it, in two independent halves

The cocycle for blades is a product of two factors, and each satisfies the cocycle equation on its own:

  Definition blade_cocycle {n : nat} (s : Signature F n)
      (v w : Vector.t bool n) : F := bsign v w * sigprod s (band v w).

bsign v w is the transposition sign and sigprod s (band v w) is the product of the signature over the shared generators, band being intersection. The sign half reduces to a statement about parities:

  Lemma bsign_cocycle : forall (n : nat) (u v w : Vector.t bool n),
    (bsign u v : F) * bsign (bxor u v) w == bsign v w * bsign u (bxor v w).
  Proof.
    intros n u v w. unfold bsign.
    rewrite <- !parity_sign_xor, (bpar_cocycle n u v w). reflexivity.
  Qed.

bsign is (−1)(-1) raised to a parity bit, so a product of two signs is the sign of the exclusive-or of the two bits — that is parity_sign_xor, applied right-to-left to turn both products into single signs. What remains is bpar_cocycle, the same equation one level down in the booleans, where it is a count rather than a sign.

The two halves then combine without interacting:

  Lemma blade_cocycle_equation : forall (n : nat) (s : Signature F n)
      (u v w : Vector.t bool n),
    blade_cocycle s u v * blade_cocycle s (u + v) w
    == blade_cocycle s v w * blade_cocycle s u (v + w).
  Proof.
    intros n s u v w. unfold blade_cocycle.
    rewrite <- !(bxor_is_add n).
    apply pair_mul; [ exact (bsign_cocycle n u v w)
                    | exact (sigprod_cocycle n s u v w) ].
  Qed.

pair_mul is the arithmetic step that lets the two halves be proved separately: from ab=cdab = cd and pq=rtpq = rt it concludes (ap)(bq)=(cr)(dt)(ap)(bq) = (cr)(dt). So the whole content is bsign_cocycle and sigprod_cocycle, and the normalizations are one line each — the empty set shares nothing with anything, and no transposition is needed against it.

Associativity, then, is a citation

twist’s associativity is proved once, for every group and every cocycle, by substituting a=b+ca = b + c in the double sum and appealing to cocycle_equation. Nothing in that proof knows what a blade is. The concrete product inherits it:

  Lemma cl_mul_assoc : forall n (s : Signature F n) (x y z : Cl F n),
    cl_mul s (cl_mul s x y) z == cl_mul s x (cl_mul s y z).
  Proof.
    intros n s x y z.
    calc (cl_mul s (cl_mul s x y) z)
      == (twist (blade_cocycle s) (cl_mul s x y) z)
           { (twist_is_cl_mul n s (cl_mul s x y) z) }
      == (twist (blade_cocycle s) (twist (blade_cocycle s) x y) z)
           { (twist_proper (blade_cocycle s)
                _ _ (symmetry (twist_is_cl_mul n s x y))
                _ _ (reflexivity z)) }
      == (twist (blade_cocycle s) x (twist (blade_cocycle s) y z))
           { (twist_assoc (blade_cocycle s) x y z
                (vector_blade_families_proper n y)
                (vector_blade_families_proper n z)) }
      == (twist (blade_cocycle s) x (cl_mul s y z))
           { (twist_proper (blade_cocycle s)
                _ _ (reflexivity x)
                _ _ (twist_is_cl_mul n s y z)) }
      == (cl_mul s x (cl_mul s y z))
           { (twist_is_cl_mul n s x (cl_mul s y z)) }.
  Qed.

Each == is one step and the braces name its justification. twist_is_cl_mul is the theorem that the two products are the same function; the first step rewrites the concrete product into the convolution, the middle step is the general associativity, and the last two rewrite back. The two vector_blade_families_proper arguments say that a coefficient family respects blade equality, which at a carrier of bit rows holds of every function.

The proof mentions no transposition and performs no induction. The whole geometric content sits in bsign_cocycle and sigprod_cocycle, where it is a statement about counting.

Computing with it

The cocycle is a function, so it evaluates. On a Euclidean signature in three dimensions, e1 and e2 in that order need no transposition and share no generator, and reversing them costs one:

  Example cocycle_e1_e2 : blade_cocycle euclidean_signature e1 e2 = (1 : Q).
  Proof. vm_compute. reflexivity. Qed.
  Example cocycle_e2_e1 : blade_cocycle euclidean_signature e2 e1 = (-1)%Q.
  Proof. vm_compute. reflexivity. Qed.

And the convolution computes the product it is the coefficient of — here e1e1e_1 e_1, read at the empty blade, which is s1=1s_1 = 1:

  Example e1_squared_euclidean :
    twist (blade_cocycle euclidean_signature)
      (blade_family e1) (blade_family e1) 0 = (1 : Q).
  Proof. vm_compute. reflexivity. Qed.

Change euclidean_signature, which is Vector.const (1 : Q) 3, to one with a −1-1 entry and the same term computes the other sign. The signature is data; the associativity above does not depend on it, because the cocycle equation holds for every signature.
