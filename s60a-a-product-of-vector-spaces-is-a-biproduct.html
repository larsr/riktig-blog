A product of vector spaces is a biproduct, and a matrix product is one equation

A matrix is a table, and everything one does with tables is indexed: the (i,j)(i,j) entry, the sum over kk in row-times-column, the block at rows ii through i′i'. Yet the facts being computed — this map decomposes into that pair of maps, composing two maps multiplies their tables — do not mention indices when stated in words. The question: how much of matrix algebra can be written with no index anywhere, over a pair of spaces rather than a numbered family? The answer is: all of it that this post touches, because a product of two vector spaces is simultaneously a product and a coproduct — a biproduct — and the four structural maps that come with that are exactly what a 2×22 \times 2 block decomposition records.

Four maps and two pairings

The carrier is the ordinary pair type A * B, with componentwise operations — the free-constructions post checks it is a vector space. A linear map here is a LinearMap, a function bundled with its linearity proofs, as built in the maps-form-a-vector-space post. From Theory/Biproduct.v: the product side gives two projections,

  Definition exl : LinearMap F (A * B) A :=
    linear_map_from_laws fst exl_proper
      (fun p q => reflexivity (fst p + fst q))
      (fun a p => reflexivity (a *: fst p)).

(and exr with snd), and the coproduct side gives two injections that pad with a zero:

  Definition inl : LinearMap F A (A * B) :=
    linear_map_from_laws (fun a : A => (a, (0 : B))) inl_proper
      (fun a a' => conj (reflexivity (a + a')) (symmetry (left_identity (0 : B))))
      (fun c a => conj (reflexivity (c *: a)) (symmetry (scale_zero c))).

The zero is what makes this linear-algebra-specific: in the category of sets there is no canonical element to pad with, and products and coproducts stay different objects. Here they coincide, and that coincidence is the entire subject.

Each side has its pairing operation. A map into the product runs two maps on the same argument; a map out of the product runs one map on each component and adds:

forkhk=y↦(hy,ky),joinfg=(a,b)↦fa+gb.\mathrm{fork}\,h\,k \;=\; y \mapsto (h\,y,\; k\,y), \qquad \mathrm{join}\,f\,g \;=\; (a,b) \mapsto f\,a + g\,b.

    Definition fork : LinearMap F X (A * B) :=
      linear_map_from_laws (fun x : X => (f x, g x)) fork_proper
        (fun x y => conj (linear_map_additive f x y) (linear_map_additive g x y))
        (fun c x => conj (linear_map_scale f c x) (linear_map_scale g c x)).
    Definition join : LinearMap F (A * B) X :=
      linear_map_from_laws (fun p : A * B => f (fst p) + g (snd p))
        join_proper join_add join_scale.

In block-matrix language, fork h k is the column (hk)\begin{pmatrix} h \\ k \end{pmatrix} and join f g is the row (fg)\begin{pmatrix} f & g \end{pmatrix} — but no matrix has been defined, and none will be.

The laws

Each pairing is characterized by its side’s structural maps. For fork, composing with a projection recovers a component, and any map into the product is the fork of its components — both by reflexivity, since the two sides are convertible:

  Theorem exl_fork : forall (f : LinearMap F X A) (g : LinearMap F X B),
    exl ∘ fork f g == f.
  Proof. intros f g x. reflexivity. Qed.

with exr_fork and fork_unique alongside. For join the laws cost one step each, because the padding zero has to be absorbed. On the blackboard, at any aa:

(joinfg)(inla)=fa+g0=fa+0=fa,(\mathrm{join}\,f\,g)(\mathrm{inl}\;a) \;=\; f\,a + g\,0 \;=\; f\,a + 0 \;=\; f\,a,

using that a linear map sends 00 to 00, then the identity law. In Coq, as a calc chain (the calc post explains the notation):

  Theorem join_inl : forall (f : LinearMap F A X) (g : LinearMap F B X),
    join f g ∘ inl == f.
  Proof.
    intros f g a.
    calc (f a + g 0)
      == (f a + (0 : X))  { (linear_map_zero g) }
      == (f a)            { right_identity }.
  Qed.

The uniqueness direction says every map out of a product is the join of its two restrictions — the 2×22 \times 2 case of “a matrix is determined by its columns.” At a pair pp:

h(p1,0)+h(0,p2)=h((p1,0)+(0,p2))=h(p),h(p_1, 0) + h(0, p_2) \;=\; h\big((p_1,0) + (0,p_2)\big) \;=\; h(p),

by additivity of hh and then the identity laws inside the pair:

  Theorem join_unique : forall h : LinearMap F (A * B) X,
    join (h ∘ inl) (h ∘ inr) == h.
  Proof.
    intros h p.
    calc (h (fst p, (0 : B)) + h ((0 : A), snd p))
      == (h ((fst p, (0 : B)) + ((0 : A), snd p)))
           { linear_map_additive }
      == (h p)
           { (linear_map_proper h _ p
                (conj (right_identity (fst p)) (left_identity (snd p)))) }.
  Qed.

Row times column

Now compose a row with a column. This is the equation matrix multiplication computes at every entry — and here it holds with no sum over an index, because the sum is the join:

joinfg∘forkhk=f∘h+g∘k.\mathrm{join}\,f\,g \circ \mathrm{fork}\,h\,k \;=\; f \circ h \,+\, g \circ k.

  Theorem join_fork : forall (f : LinearMap F A X) (g : LinearMap F B X)
      (h : LinearMap F Y A) (k : LinearMap F Y B),
    join f g ∘ fork h k == f ∘ h + g ∘ k.
  Proof. intros f g h k y. reflexivity. Qed.

The proof is reflexivity: apply both sides to yy and each computes to f(hy)+g(ky)f(h\,y) + g(k\,y). The addition on the right-hand side is addition of linear maps — the pointwise vector-space structure on Hom\mathrm{Hom} from the maps-form-a-vector-space post. A full 2×22\times 2 block product is this equation applied at each of the four positions of join-of-forks composed with fork-of-joins; an n×mn \times m one is the same equation at every splitting. What the indexed formula sums with Σk\Sigma_k, the structural presentation carries in the associativity of +.

Representation is conjugation

The file ends where the representations post starts, and sharpens it. To represent an abstract map f:U→Vf : U \to V concretely, choose an isomorphism on each side — that is what choosing a basis does — and conjugate; VSIso is the recorded pair of mutually inverse linear maps from the curry post:

  Definition rep (i : VSIso F U P) (j : VSIso F V Q) (f : LinearMap F U V)
    : LinearMap F P Q :=
    VSIso.to j ∘ f ∘ VSIso.from i.

That composing maps multiplies their representations — the fact Theory/MatRep.v proves entrywise, as a sum of products of coordinates — is here one cancellation: in tok∘g∘fromj∘toj∘f∘fromi\mathrm{to}_k \circ g \circ \mathrm{from}_j \circ \mathrm{to}_j \circ f \circ \mathrm{from}_i, the middle round trip disappears,

  Theorem rep_comp : forall (i : VSIso F U P) (j : VSIso F V Q) (k : VSIso F W R)
      (g : LinearMap F V W) (f : LinearMap F U V),
    rep i k (g ∘ f) == rep j k g ∘ rep i j f.
  Proof.
    intros i j k g f p. unfold rep. simpl. symmetry.
    calc (VSIso.to k (g (VSIso.from j (VSIso.to j (f (VSIso.from i p))))))
      == (VSIso.to k (g (f (VSIso.from i p))))
           { vsiso_from_to }.
  Qed.

one calc step, vsiso_from_to. The indexed and the structural accounts prove the same theorem; the structural one shows what the sum over kk was doing all along — cancelling a change of basis against its inverse.
