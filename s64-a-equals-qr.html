A = QR, for real

Take nn vectors and make them mutually perpendicular without changing what they span. The recipe is old and short: walk the list from left to right, and from each new vector subtract its projection onto every vector already produced, keeping only the part that points in a direction none of the earlier ones covered. The projection of uu onto the axis through ww is ⟨u,w⟩⟨w,w⟩w\frac{\langle u,w\rangle}{\langle w,w\rangle}w, so each subtraction is a scalar multiple of an earlier output.

That last remark is the whole of what follows. Because every subtraction is a multiple of an earlier output, each original vector can be rebuilt from the outputs by a combination that uses only outputs at its own position or before it. Arrange the original vectors as the columns of a matrix AA and the perpendicular ones as the columns of a matrix QQ, and the table of rebuild coefficients is a third matrix RR with

A=QR,Rij=0 whenever i>jA = Q\,R, \qquad R_{ij} = 0 \text{ whenever } i > j

— zero below the diagonal, which is what upper triangular means. The factorization is not a new construction; it is bookkeeping about one that already exists. The question worth asking is what has to be proved to earn the two claims: that the product really is AA, and that the entries below the diagonal really are zero rather than merely small or conventionally ignored.

The three matrices

Fix a space VV with a positive-definite pairing, a basis B of size n so that vectors can be written in coordinates, and a family of n vectors v : Fin.t n -> V (Fin.t n being the type of indices 0,…,n−10,\dots,n-1). Write gram_schmidt_fin v for the perpendicularized family. Then all three matrices are one line each (Impl/QR.v):

  Definition qr_Q (v : Fin.t n -> V) : Matrix n n F :=
    fun i j => basis_coords B (gram_schmidt_fin v j) i.

  Definition qr_R_coeff (v : Fin.t n -> V) (j k : Fin.t n) : F :=
    if lt_dec (fin_to_nat k) (fin_to_nat j)
      then ⟨ v j , gram_schmidt_fin v k ⟩ * inv (norm2 (gram_schmidt_fin v k)) else 0.

  Definition qr_R (v : Fin.t n -> V) : Matrix n n F :=
    fun i j => e j i + qr_R_coeff v j i.

  Definition qr_A (v : Fin.t n -> V) : Matrix n n F :=
    fun i j => basis_coords B (v j) i.

basis_coords B w i is coordinate i of the vector w in the chosen basis, so column j of qr_Q is the j-th perpendicularized vector written out in coordinates, and column j of qr_A is the j-th original one. The entry qr_R v i j has two summands. The second, qr_R_coeff, is the projection coefficient of v j onto the i-th output, gated by the test fin_to_nat i < fin_to_nat j: it is the coefficient when the output is strictly earlier than the column, and zero otherwise. The gate is where “only earlier vectors were subtracted” enters the matrix. The first summand is the Kronecker delta from the standard basis,

  Definition e {n : nat} (i : Fin.t n) : Fin.t n -> F :=
    fun j => if dec (i = j) then 1 else 0.

so e j i contributes a 11 exactly on the diagonal.

Upper triangular, proved

Statement. Below the diagonal, qr_R is zero.

On the blackboard, read the two summands separately at an entry with j<ij < i. The Kronecker delta e j i is zero because jj and ii are different indices — they cannot be equal, since one is strictly smaller than the other. And the gated coefficient is zero because its gate asks whether i<ji < j, which is false when j<ij < i: an ordering cannot hold both ways. Zero plus zero is zero.

  Theorem qr_R_upper : forall (v : Fin.t n -> V) (i j : Fin.t n),
    (fin_to_nat j < fin_to_nat i)%nat -> qr_R v i j == 0.
  Proof.
    intros v i j Hji.
    assert (Hne : j <> i).
    { intros Heq. rewrite Heq in Hji. exact (Nat.lt_irrefl (fin_to_nat i) Hji). }
    unfold qr_R, qr_R_coeff.
    rewrite (e_at_neq n j i Hne).
    destruct (lt_dec (fin_to_nat i) (fin_to_nat j)) as [Hlt | _].
    - destruct (Nat.lt_asymm (fin_to_nat j) (fin_to_nat i) Hji Hlt).
    - exact (left_identity (0 : F)).
  Qed.

Walking it: the assert establishes j <> i — if they were equal, the hypothesis Hji would say an index is strictly less than itself, which Nat.lt_irrefl refutes. unfold exposes the two summands, and e_at_neq rewrites the delta at distinct indices to 0. The destruct runs the gate’s decision: in the branch where the gate fires we have both j<ij < i and i<ji < j, which Nat.lt_asymm turns into a proof of False, closing that branch; in the branch where it does not, the entry is 0 + 0, and left_identity — zero is a left identity for addition — finishes.

The diagonal is one, not the length

Statement. On the diagonal, qr_R is 1.

This is where the factorization differs from the picture in most courses, where RR’s diagonal holds the lengths of the perpendicularized vectors and QQ’s columns come out with length one. The reason for the difference is worth stating plainly: normalizing a vector means dividing by its length, and length is a square root. No square root is available over an unspecified ordered field, so the construction here never normalizes — qr_Q’s columns are the raw perpendicularized vectors, orthogonal but not of unit length.

Given that, the diagonal is forced. Each original vector satisfies

vj=(the j-th output)+(a combination of strictly earlier outputs),v_j \;=\; \bigl(\text{the } j\text{-th output}\bigr) \;+\; \bigl(\text{a combination of strictly earlier outputs}\bigr),

which is gram_schmidt_fin_reconstruct, and matching the coefficient of the jj-th output on both sides of A=QRA = QR leaves exactly 11. The proof is again two summands read at one entry: the delta at equal indices is 11, and the gate asks whether i<ii < i, which is false, so the coefficient is 00.

  Theorem qr_R_diag : forall (v : Fin.t n -> V) (i : Fin.t n), qr_R v i i == 1.
  Proof.
    intros v i.
    unfold qr_R, qr_R_coeff.
    rewrite (e_at_eq n i i eq_refl).
    destruct (lt_dec (fin_to_nat i) (fin_to_nat i)) as [Hlt | _].
    - destruct (Nat.lt_irrefl (fin_to_nat i) Hlt).
    - exact (right_identity (1 : F)).
  Qed.

Walking it: e_at_eq rewrites the delta at equal indices to 1; the destruct on the gate discards the impossible branch with Nat.lt_irrefl; and right_identity — zero is a right identity — turns 1 + 0 into 1.

One consequence comes for free and is stronger than the textbook version. The diagonal is nonzero unconditionally: it is the multiplicative unit, and 1≠01 \ne 0 is part of what being a field means, so no hypothesis about the input vectors is needed at all.

  Theorem qr_R_diag_nonzero : forall (v : Fin.t n -> V) (i : Fin.t n),
    qr_R v i i =/= 0.
  Proof.
    intros v i Hcontra.
    apply (nontrivial (F:=F)).
    calc (1 : F)
      == (qr_R v i i) { (qr_R_diag v i) }
      == (0 : F)      { Hcontra }.
  Qed.

The proof reads as a contradiction: assume the diagonal entry is 0; then 1 == qr_R v i i == 0 by qr_R_diag and that assumption, and Field.nontrivial — the field axiom 1 =/= 0 — refutes it.

The factorization itself

Statement. Provided every perpendicularized vector has nonzero squared length, AA is the product QRQ\,R.

  Theorem qr_factorization : forall (v : Fin.t n -> V),
    (forall k : Fin.t n, norm2 (gram_schmidt_fin v k) =/= 0) ->
    qr_A v == mat_mul (qr_Q v) (qr_R v).

The hypothesis is not decoration. qr_R_coeff divides by norm2 (gram_schmidt_fin v k), so unless that is nonzero the coefficients are not the reconstruction coefficients and the product is not AA. Constructively, “every output has nonzero squared length” is what “the input family was linearly independent” amounts to: if some input were a combination of the earlier ones, subtracting the projections would leave nothing behind. Notice where the hypothesis is not needed — the two triangularity theorems above take none, because they are facts about the shape of qr_R, not about what it reconstructs.

The proof reads A=QRA = QR off gram_schmidt_fin_reconstruct one basis coordinate at a time: entry (i,j)(i,j) of QRQ R is a sum over kk of QikRkjQ_{ik}R_{kj}, which is coordinate ii of the combination of outputs that gram_schmidt_fin_reconstruct says rebuilds v j, and coordinate ii of v j is entry (i,j)(i,j) of AA by definition.

Why the construction was re-expressed for Fin.t n

There is a design point buried in gram_schmidt_fin that is easy to miss. The list-based Gram–Schmidt of Theory/GramSchmidt.v already proves its output pairwise orthogonal — but it does so through membership in a list, and its conclusion is a disjunction: any two members are orthogonal or literally the same vector. A list has no notion of “the vector at position 3”, so once positions are erased into membership, “these two are different vectors” is the only way left to say “these are two different outputs” — and deciding whether two vectors are different is exactly the sort of test that is unavailable here.

Matrices need positions: column ii against column jj, with i≠ji \ne j as indices, not as vectors. So Impl/QR.v re-expresses the same recursion at Fin.t n-indexed families and proves, as a bridge lemma gram_schmidt_fin_aux_eq, that this re-expression computes the very same vectors the list version does. Every property established for the list construction then transfers across unchanged, and the orthogonality statement can be phrased on indices, without the disjunction. Changing the representation cost a bridge lemma; it cost no re-proving of the mathematics.
