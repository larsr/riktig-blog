  1. A sum is two equations, not a fold [has code: Structure/BigOp.v, Theory/BigOp.v, Impl/BigopFold.v, Impl/BigopAcc.v, Lib/BigopList.v] — written 2026-08-18 as blog/s100-a-sum-is-two-equations.md. 61b. Two representations of a set, and a theorem only one of them satisfies [has code: Structure/Blade.v, Impl/BladeVector.v, Impl/BladeMask.v, Impl/BladeEnum.v, Impl/Clifford.v] — written 2026-08-19 as blog/s101-two-representations-of-a-set.md. The problem: a subset of n generators can be stored as a row of n bits or as one binary number, and the second has slack — at width 1 the numbers 0 and 2 store the same subset, since bit 1 is not part of it. Can any statement about subsets notice? The formalization: Blade, membership plus the equations carrying the empty subset, the full one, symmetric difference and intersection to the bit operation of the same name, with member_faithful saying that subsets with the same members are equal; then the two setoid equalities, positions for the row and first-n-bits for the number. The proof: vector_bool_equ_eq (agreement at every position IS row equality, via Vector.eq_nth_iff), hence vector_blade_families_proper — every function out of the rows respects subset equality, assuming nothing about the function — against word_families_are_not_all_proper, which refutes the same statement at the number carrier with fun x => N.eqb x 0. The usage: bsum_reindex, reindexing a sum over all 2^n subsets by symmetric difference with a fixed one, whose general reindexing lemma wants the summand to respect subset equality and gets it free at the row carrier — so the statement carries no hypothesis about the summand, which at the number carrier it would have to. The lesson the post ends on: theorems stated in terms of membership hold at both representations, and part company exactly when a statement quantifies over functions out of the carrier.
