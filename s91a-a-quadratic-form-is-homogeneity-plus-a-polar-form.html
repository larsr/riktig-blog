A quadratic form is degree-two homogeneity plus a bilinear polar form

Length-squared behaves unlike length. Doubling a vector quadruples it, and it fails additivity in a structured way: the error term |u+v|2−|u|2−|v|2|u+v|^2 - |u|^2 - |v|^2 is not junk but 2⟨u,v⟩2\,\langle u,v\rangle, the inner product in disguise. A quadratic form is the axiomatization of exactly this behavior: a scalar-valued map that scales by the square of the scalar, and whose additivity defect is as well-behaved as possible. The interest is not only metric — a quadratic form is the one parameter a geometric algebra needs, and the forms with negative squares, which no inner product provides, are the ones relativity runs on.

The structure

The additivity defect gets a name first — the polarization of QQ, from Structure/QuadraticForm.v:

Definition polar {F V : Type} `{VectorSpace F V} (Q : V -> F) (u v : V) : F :=
  Q (u + v) + - Q u + - Q v.

polarQ(u,v)=Q(u+v)−Q(u)−Q(v).\mathrm{polar}\,Q\,(u,v) \;=\; Q(u+v) - Q(u) - Q(v).

Then the class: QQ respects the carrier’s equality, is homogeneous of degree two, and its polar form is linear in the first argument —

Q(av)=a2Q(v),u↦polarQ(u,v) linear for each v.Q(a\,v) = a^2\, Q(v), \qquad u \mapsto \mathrm{polar}\,Q\,(u,v) \ \text{ linear for each } v.

  Class QuadraticForm (F V : Type) `{VectorSpace F V} (Q : V -> F) := {
    quad_proper    :: Proper (equ ==> equ) Q;
    is_homogeneous :: Homogeneous F V F mult (fun a => a * a) Q;
    polar_linear   :: forall v : V, Linear F V F (fun u => polar Q u v);
  }.

The homogeneity field reuses the one-law Homogeneous class of the linearity post, instantiated at the weight a↦a⋅aa \mapsto a \cdot a — the same class that says “linear” when the weight is the identity says “quadratic” here. Linearity in the second argument of the polar form is not assumed: polar is visibly symmetric (Q(u+v)Q(u+v) does not care about the order), so one slot suffices, and the polar form is bilinear.

Three instances, one proof pattern

The field itself. The one-dimensional case is squaring, Q(u)=u2Q(u) = u^2, from Theory/QuadraticForm.v:

  Definition square_form : F -> F := fun u => u * u.

  Lemma polar_square : forall u v : F, polar square_form u v == u * v + u * v.
  Proof.
    intros u v. unfold polar, square_form. ring.
  Qed.

The blackboard computation behind that ring call is the binomial one: (u+v)2−u2−v2=2uv(u+v)^2 - u^2 - v^2 = 2uv, written uv+uvuv + uv because nothing here assumes 22 exists as a scalar — the same characteristic-two caution as in the geometric-product post.

An inner product. In an inner product space, length-squared is norm2, Q(u)=⟨u,u⟩Q(u) = \langle u, u\rangle, and the polar form recovers the doubled inner product. On the blackboard:

⟨u+v,u+v⟩−⟨u,u⟩−⟨v,v⟩=⟨u,v⟩+⟨v,u⟩=⟨u,v⟩+⟨u,v⟩,\langle u+v,\, u+v\rangle - \langle u,u\rangle - \langle v,v\rangle \;=\; \langle u,v\rangle + \langle v,u\rangle \;=\; \langle u,v\rangle + \langle u,v\rangle,

expanding by bilinearity, then commuting the second term. In Coq:

  Lemma polar_norm2 : forall u v : V, polar norm2 u v == ⟪ u , v ⟫ + ⟪ u , v ⟫.
  Proof.
    intros u v. unfold polar.
    rewrite (norm2_add u v), <- (commutative u v). ring.
  Qed.

norm2_add is the expansion of ⟨u+v,u+v⟩\langle u+v, u+v\rangle, commutative swaps ⟨v,u⟩\langle v,u\rangle, and ring cancels — the polarization identity of the polarization post, reappearing as the statement that norm2 is a quadratic form (Inner_QuadraticForm packages it, with the degree-two law proved by pulling the scalar out of each slot in turn).

The negation. If QQ is a quadratic form, so is −Q-Q (neg_form, instance Neg_QuadraticForm):

  Definition neg_form : V -> F := fun u => - Q u.

An inner product cannot do this — −⟨u,u⟩-\langle u,u \rangle is negative somewhere unless everything is zero — and that is the point: the quadratic-form axioms keep exactly the structure that survives sign changes, which is why metric signatures mixing +1+1s and −1-1s fit where “inner product” would be false.

What it is for

A geometric algebra is an algebra over a quadratic space — a vector space carrying a chosen quadratic form — and the form enters the axioms in exactly one place, the contraction law of Structure/GeometricAlgebra.v:

    contraction : forall u : V, ga_vec u * ga_vec u == Q u *: 1;

every vector squares to its own quadratic value. The whole class is parameterized by a QuadraticForm: instantiate QQ as norm2 and the algebra is Euclidean, as the all-ones signature of the grading post’s Clifford implementation; flip signs with neg_form-style entries — the sig_pq signatures with pp generators squaring to +1+1 and qq to −1-1 — and the same theorems serve the spacetime algebra of the Maxwell post. One structure, three instance families, and the sign flexibility is not an accommodation but a field of the design.
