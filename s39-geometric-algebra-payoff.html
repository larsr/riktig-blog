Multiplying vectors: the contraction axiom, and the complex numbers as a Clifford algebra

A geometric algebra embeds a vector space VV into a ring AA — a place where vectors can be not only added but multiplied, with the multiplication distributing over addition but not required to commute. The one governing law is that a vector times itself collapses to a scalar, its own squared length: v⋅v=Q(v)⋅1v\cdot v = Q(v) \cdot 1, where QQ is the quadratic form measuring squared length, and the scalar acts on the ring’s unit. From that single axiom the polarization identity falls out — the symmetric part of the product of two vectors recovers the cross term of QQ,

uv+vu=polar⁡Q(u,v)⋅1,polar⁡Q(u,v)=Q(u+v)−Q(u)−Q(v),u\,v + v\,u \;=\; \operatorname{polar} Q(u,v)\cdot 1, \qquad \operatorname{polar} Q(u,v) = Q(u+v) - Q(u) - Q(v),

and when the squared length comes from an inner product, Q(v)=⟨v,v⟩Q(v) = \langle v,v\rangle, that cross term is ⟨u,v⟩+⟨u,v⟩\langle u,v\rangle + \langle u,v\rangle.

That identity is the subject of an earlier post; here it is the fact we build on rather than re-prove. This post is about what the product is for, and about the smallest concrete algebra in which the axiom actually holds.

The picture: reflections and rotations as products

The reason to multiply vectors is that rigid motions become multiplication. Read the polarization identity when uu and vv are orthogonal: their inner product vanishes, so the cross term is 00 and uv+vu=0uv + vu = 0, i.e. uv=−vuuv = -vu — orthogonal vectors anticommute. And a unit vector vv, one with Q(v)=1Q(v) = 1, is its own inverse in the ring, since vv=1v\,v = 1.

Put those together. For a unit vector vv, the map

x↦−vxvx \;\longmapsto\; -\,v\,x\,v

reflects xx across the hyperplane orthogonal to vv: the component of xx along vv anticommutes past one factor and gets negated twice into a flip, while the components orthogonal to vv commute through and are left alone. Compose two such reflections and the double sign cancels; what remains, x↦RxR−1x \mapsto R\,x\,R^{-1} for a product R=vwR = vw of two unit vectors, is a rotation — the angle doubled from the angle between the two mirrors. This is the whole reason geometric algebra exists: the awkward, dimension-specific formulas for reflections and rotations become one uniform sandwich product, and RR is a single object, a rotor, that composes by ordinary multiplication.

Two boundaries, stated plainly. First, the reflection and rotation theorems above are the motivating picture for the subject in general; they are not formalized in the files this post draws from, and this post does not claim otherwise. Second, anticommutation only has room to act when there are two independent directions, dimension ≥2\ge 2. What the files do contain is the axiom itself, the polarization identity drawn from it, and the smallest concrete algebra satisfying the axiom — the case where “a vector squared is minus its length” is exactly the equation i2=−1i^2 = -1.

The concrete algebra: Cl(0,1)\mathrm{Cl}(0,1) is the complex numbers

Take a one-dimensional space of vectors — a line, modelled by the field FF itself — and give it the negated square as its quadratic form, Q(u)=−(uu)Q(u) = -(u\,u), which is the squared length of the negative inner product ⟨u,v⟩=−(uv)\langle u,v\rangle = -(u\,v). Embed the line into the complex numbers ℂF\mathbb{C}F (pairs over FF, a ring for any field) along the imaginary axis: vec(u)=0+ui\mathrm{vec}(u) = 0 + u\,i. Then the governing axiom asks for

vec(u)⋅vec(u)=Q(u)⋅1,\mathrm{vec}(u)\cdot\mathrm{vec}(u) \;=\; Q(u) \cdot 1,

and both sides are computable. Componentwise, the complex multiplication rule gives

(0+ui)(0+ui)=(0⋅0−uu)+(0⋅u+u⋅0)i=−u2+0i,(0 + u\,i)(0 + u\,i) \;=\; (0\cdot 0 - u\,u) + (0\cdot u + u\cdot 0)\,i \;=\; -u^2 + 0\,i,

while the right-hand side is Q(u)=−(uu)=−u2Q(u) = -(u\,u) = -u^2, scaled onto the real unit. They match — and that matching is i2=−1i^2 = -1, now read as “the unit vector on the imaginary axis squares to the negative of its length.” The two component equations are isolated as plain field-level facts first.

Lemma cliff1_contract_re : forall u : F,
  0 * 0 + - (u * u) == (- (u * u)) * 1.
Proof. intros u. ring. Qed.

Lemma cliff1_contract_im : forall u : F,
  0 * u + u * 0 == (- (u * u)) * 0.
Proof. intros u. ring. Qed.

The real part is the multiplication rule of ℂF\mathbb{C}F applied to (0,u)⋅(0,u)(0,u)\cdot(0,u): real part 0⋅0−u⋅u0\cdot 0 - u\cdot u, which is −(uu)-(u u) after the 0⋅00\cdot 0 term is dropped, and that equals (−(uu))⋅1(-(u u))\cdot 1, the real component of the scalar −(uu)-(uu) sitting on the unit. The imaginary part is 0⋅u+u⋅0=00\cdot u + u\cdot 0 = 0, matching (−(uu))⋅0(-(uu))\cdot 0.

Both are equations between polynomials in one variable over the field, which is exactly what ring decides. The section is opened with

  Add Ring cliff1_ga_ring : (ring_theory_of_Ring (R := F) commutative).

handing Coq’s ring solver a ring_theory record assembled from the field’s own laws, after which each of these goals is one word: the solver normalizes both sides into a canonical polynomial and finds them identical. Written out by hand each would be a three- or four-link chain through mul_0_l, left_identity and right_identity, and there would be nothing to learn from reading it.

The contraction axiom for Cl(0,1)\mathrm{Cl}(0,1) is then those two facts, one per component of the complex number:

Lemma cliff1_ga_contract : forall u : F,
  cliff1_ga_vec u * cliff1_ga_vec u
  == InnerProductSpace.norm2 (F:=F) (V:=F) (H1:=Cliff1_NegInner) u *: 1.
Proof.
  intros u.
  split.
  - exact (cliff1_contract_re u).
  - exact (cliff1_contract_im u).
Qed.

Equality of two complex numbers is equality of their real and imaginary parts, which is what split produces; cliff1_contract_re discharges the first, cliff1_contract_im the second. That lemma is the last argument to the constructor certifying ℂF\mathbb{C}F, with vectors on the imaginary axis and the negative-definite form, as a genuine geometric algebra:

  Definition GA_Cliff1 : GeometricAlgebra F F (C F) (Q := neg_form square_form) :=
    {| GeometricAlgebra.ring         := C_Ring;
       GeometricAlgebra.scaling      := Cliff1_ScaleLaws;
       GeometricAlgebra.mul_linear_l := fun y =>
         {| Linear.proper := fun x x' Hx => op_proper x x' Hx y y (reflexivity y);
            Linear.is_additive := fun x x' => right_distributive x x' y;
            Linear.is_homogeneous := fun a x => cliff1_ga_scale_mul_l a x y |};
       GeometricAlgebra.mul_linear_r := fun x =>
         {| Linear.proper := fun y y' Hy => op_proper x x (reflexivity x) y y' Hy;
            Linear.is_additive := fun y y' => left_distributive x y y';
            Linear.is_homogeneous := fun a y => cliff1_ga_scale_mul_r a x y |};
       GeometricAlgebra.ga_vec       := cliff1_ga_vec;
       GeometricAlgebra.vec_linear :=
         {| Linear.proper         := cliff1_ga_vec_proper;
            Linear.is_additive    := cliff1_ga_vec_add;
            Linear.is_homogeneous := cliff1_ga_vec_scale |};
       GeometricAlgebra.contraction  := cliff1_ga_contract |}.

The record names no carriers of its own: GeometricAlgebra takes FF, VV and AA implicitly, and the annotation on GA_Cliff1 is where VV is fixed to the line FF, AA to ℂF\mathbb{C}F, and the quadratic form to neg_form square_form — the square u↦uuu \mapsto u\,u on a field, run through the operation that negates a quadratic form and proves the result is one.

Look at what the fields are. ring and scaling are not lists of laws being restated here: they are a Ring (C F) and a ScaleLaws F (C F), whole structures proved once elsewhere and named. Because the class declares them with ::, they go back into the instance database, so every lemma about rings applies to ℂF\mathbb{C}F read as a geometric algebra, with no second copy of the additive laws anywhere. The two mul_linear_* fields say multiplication is linear in each argument, and vec_linear says the embedding is; each is a three-line Linear literal whose fields are lemmas from the same file. Only contraction is specific to this algebra, and that is the lemma above.

Because none of this needed positivity of the inner product or any special field, the construction typechecks for an arbitrary field FF — the smoke test at the end of the file states exactly that:

Example ga_cliff1_check (F : Type) `{FF : Field F} :
  GeometricAlgebra F F (C F) (Q := neg_form square_form) := GA_Cliff1.

So the abstract axiom “a vector squared is its length” is not vacuous: there is a concrete ring satisfying it, and it is one every reader already knows.
